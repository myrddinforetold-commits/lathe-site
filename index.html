Sheet 02 / Project Madryn

Our own compute, on Patagonian wind.

Project Madryn is Lathe's first compute site: a 400 kW AI facility in Puerto Madryn, on the coast of Argentine Patagonia, with an Applied AI Lab built on top. It trains our clients' models on hardware we own, powered from beside some of Argentina's largest wind farms and cooled by Patagonian air.

Why Patagonia

Power, cold air and room to grow, in one place.

Power at the source

Wind farms on the city's doorstep and a 500 kV substation feeding the national grid. Compute goes where the power is.

Cooled by the climate

Cool, dry air does most of the cooling, so little power and little water go on chillers.

Data that stays put

Client data and training runs stay in Argentina, which the EU recognises as protecting personal data adequately.

Room to grow

Land and power for more halls on site, and a model for further sites across Patagonia.

The Applied AI Lab

Where the method meets its own hardware.

The Lab trains private models for banks, sports organisations, insurers and public bodies across Latin America and Europe. Each client brings its records; we post-train an open model on them, test it against the methods they use today, and hand over a model they own. Running on our own GPUs makes every training run cheaper and keeps client data in the country.
